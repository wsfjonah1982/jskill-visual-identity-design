# Business Types — what each needs from an identity

Use with the three required inputs (company name, industry, business type/idea) to write five
on-target ideas. For each type: what customers must feel, logo approaches that work, palette
tendencies, clichés to avoid (put them in an idea's `avoid`), what the brand story graphic
should show, and how the business card should feel.

`business_type` in `brand.json` is a short label (`tech`, `resell`, `manufacturing`,
`restaurant`, …). Anything not listed here: pick the closest row and adapt.

---

## Tech (software, SaaS, AI, apps, IT services)
- **Must feel**: capable, modern, trustworthy with data, simple to start.
- **Logos that work**: abstract or monogram symbol + clean sans; a mark that works as an app icon.
- **Palette**: one confident saturated colour (blue, violet, green, electric orange) + dark neutral; or dark-mode-first.
- **Clichés**: circuit boards, globes, generic swooshes, cloud icons, binary code, robot heads.
- **Story graphic**: problem → product moment → outcome for a real user (a person, not a screen full of fake UI).
- **Card**: minimal, lots of space, symbol on one side; matte, coloured edge, or dark card.

## Resell (retail, e-commerce, distributor, trading, marketplace, second-hand)
- **Must feel**: good value or good taste (pick one), reliable delivery, easy to buy from.
- **Logos that work**: friendly wordmark; a symbol from packaging/tags/boxes; strong single colour for packaging tape and labels.
- **Palette**: one bold ownable colour that reads on cardboard and phone screens.
- **Clichés**: shopping carts, bags with a swoosh, price tags, generic arrows, "$" symbols.
- **Story graphic**: sourcing → curation/quality check → happy customer unboxing.
- **Card**: practical, legible; kraft or bold colour; could double as a tag or thank-you insert.

## Manufacturing (factory, industrial, OEM, fabrication, engineering, B2B products)
- **Must feel**: precise, robust, dependable, scale and safety.
- **Logos that work**: solid geometric symbol (often initial-based), heavy sans or slab wordmark; must survive embossing, stencilling and machinery decals.
- **Palette**: deep blue/navy, steel grey, industrial orange or safety yellow accent.
- **Clichés**: gears, cogs, wrenches, generic factory silhouettes, lightning bolts.
- **Story graphic**: raw material → precise process (machines, skilled hands) → finished product in use.
- **Card**: sturdy heavy stock, maybe metallic ink or blind emboss; structured grid.

## Restaurant (restaurant, café, bar, bakery, food truck, F&B brand)
- **Must feel**: appetite, atmosphere, the cuisine's origin and price level at a glance.
- **Logos that work**: wordmark with personality, badge/emblem (heritage), playful hand-lettering (casual), refined minimal mark (fine dining); must work on signage, cups, aprons, menus.
- **Palette**: warm appetising tones (reds, oranges, mustard, cream) or cuisine-specific; avoid cold blue-grey unless intentional.
- **Clichés**: chef hats, crossed fork and knife, steaming bowls, generic flames (unless reinvented).
- **Story graphic**: origin of the food/recipe → the cooking → people enjoying it together.
- **Card**: tactile — textured, letterpress, kraft, rounded corners; often doubles as a loyalty card.

## Professional services (law, accounting, consulting, finance, insurance)
- **Must feel**: credible, discreet, competent, stable.
- **Logos that work**: refined wordmark or monogram; restrained symbol.
- **Palette**: navy, deep green, burgundy, charcoal + one warm accent.
- **Clichés**: scales of justice, columns, handshake, upward arrows, pie charts.
- **Story graphic**: client challenge → expert guidance → confident outcome.
- **Card**: premium, classic, thick stock, maybe foil.

## Health, wellness & beauty (clinic, spa, fitness, skincare, salon)
- **Must feel**: care, calm or energy (fitness), cleanliness, results.
- **Logos that work**: soft symbol from a natural/body form, elegant or rounded type.
- **Palette**: soft naturals, sage, clay, blush, or clean white + one clinical colour.
- **Clichés**: lotus, leaves, generic silhouettes, medical crosses, hearts.
- **Story graphic**: how a customer feels before → the treatment/ritual → after.
- **Card**: soft-touch, uncoated, rounded corners; appointment space on the back.

## Education & childcare
- **Must feel**: growth, safety, curiosity, trust for parents.
- **Logos**: friendly symbol, rounded type, works in many colours.
- **Clichés**: graduation caps, books, apples, ABC blocks.
- **Story graphic**: learner's starting point → learning moment → achievement.

## Real estate, construction & architecture
- **Must feel**: solid, aspirational, local expertise.
- **Logos**: monogram, structural geometric mark, strong sans or refined serif.
- **Clichés**: roof outlines, house + key, skyline silhouettes, hard hats.
- **Story graphic**: site/plan → building → people living/working in the space.

## Logistics & transport
- **Must feel**: speed, reliability, reach.
- **Logos**: forward-moving geometric mark, bold italic or wide sans; must read on vehicles at distance.
- **Clichés**: speed lines, globes, arrows around a box.
- **Story graphic**: pickup → moving network → on-time delivery.

## Creative & agency (design, marketing, media, events)
- **Must feel**: original, confident taste.
- **Logos**: expressive wordmark, flexible system; it's fine to be bold.
- **Clichés**: lightbulbs, pencils, paint splashes, speech bubbles.
- **Story graphic**: the client's brief → the idea → the work in the world.

---

## Making the five ideas different (summary — see `design-ideas.md`)

For any type, the five ideas should span: one **category-classic** (what customers expect,
done really well), one **modern/clean**, one **bold differentiator**, one **premium/refined**,
and one **warm/playful or local-culture** direction — adjusted to what makes sense for the
business (no playful idea for a law firm unless the brief asks for it).
